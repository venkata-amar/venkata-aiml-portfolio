The Evolution of AI and Machine Learning: A Visual Timeline

Created for AIML-500: Machine Learning Fundamentals, Workshop One.

Introduction
As part of my Machine Learning Fundamentals course, I created a visual timeline tracing the history of artificial intelligence from 1950 to today.
Description
This artifact is a landscape formatted infographic that maps major milestones in AI history, including the Turing Test, the Dartmouth Conference, the two AI Winters, and the recent deep learning breakthroughs that led to tools like ChatGPT.
Objective
Research and visually organize the key turning points in AI history, understanding not just what happened but why certain periods saw rapid progress while others saw funding and interest collapse.
Process
Researched historical milestones using academic and industry sources, organized them into three categories (general milestones, AI winters, recent breakthroughs), then used AI assistance to help draft and refine the visual layout, which I reviewed and finalized myself.
Value Proposition
Unique Value
Demonstrates the ability to take a broad, complex topic and distill it into something clear and visually accessible for a non-technical audience.
Relevance
Understanding AI's historical hype cycles and funding winters gives useful context for evaluating today's AI boom with realistic expectations, valuable for any employer investing in AI/ML initiatives.

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning: A Comparative Case Study

Created for AIML-500: Machine Learning Fundamentals, Workshop Two.

Excerpt
"These two examples illustrate the core distinction between machine learning and deep learning: traditional machine learning works best when the important features are already known and the data is structured and limited, while deep learning is suited to large volumes of raw, unstructured data where the model must discover its own patterns. Choosing the right approach is not about which method is more advanced, but about matching the complexity of the tool to the complexity of the problem."
Download Full Report (.docx)
Introduction
As part of my Machine Learning Fundamentals course, I wrote a comparative case study analyzing when to use traditional machine learning versus deep learning, grounded in two real-world scenarios.
Description
This report examines a Support Vector Machine used for telecom customer churn prediction alongside a Convolutional Neural Network used in autonomous vehicle systems, explaining why each modeling approach fits its problem and why the alternative would not.
Objective
Demonstrate the ability to reason about which class of machine learning approach fits a given business or engineering problem, rather than defaulting to the most advanced available technique.
Process
Selected one structured-data example and one unstructured-data example, researched real-world applications for each, and built a structured argument comparing data type, feature extraction, interpretability, and computing cost across both approaches, including a summary comparison table.
Value Proposition
Unique Value
Shows that I can evaluate a technical approach against the actual shape of a problem, structured versus unstructured data, interpretability needs, and cost, rather than assuming more complex models are always better.
Relevance
This kind of judgment is directly relevant to my work as a data engineer, where choosing the right tool for a pipeline or model has real cost and maintainability consequences for the business.
Artifact 3 of 5

Explaining Neural Networks Through Everyday Analogy

Created for AIML-500: Machine Learning Fundamentals, Workshop Three.

Excerpt
"Imagine a big group of friends trying to guess what's in a wrapped box. No one friend knows for sure, but each friend has one clue. Each shouts out a guess based on their one clue, and a judge combines them into one final answer. That's basically what a neural network is: a bunch of tiny decision-makers, each looking at one small piece of information, passing their opinion forward until the whole system agrees on a final answer."
Introduction
As part of a discussion on communicating AI concepts to non-technical audiences, I wrote a plain-language explanation of how neural networks learn, built entirely around an everyday analogy.
Description
This piece explains neural networks and the concept of weight adjustment during training using a story about a group of friends guessing the contents of a box, where each friend represents a neuron contributing one piece of evidence to a final decision.
Objective
Practice translating a technical AI concept into language accessible to a non-technical audience, applying specific communication strategies such as analogy, chunking information into digestible sections, and progressive disclosure from simple to more technical detail.
Process
Selected a relatable, everyday scenario to mirror how a neural network combines many small pieces of evidence, structured the explanation into three clearly labeled sections, and closed by connecting the analogy back to recognizable real-world applications like photo recognition and spam filtering.
Value Proposition
Unique Value
Demonstrates the ability to make complex AI concepts approachable for non-technical stakeholders, without sacrificing accuracy about how the underlying system actually works.
Relevance
This is a core skill for any data or AI professional who needs leadership, clients, or cross-functional teams to trust and understand AI systems well enough to support their adoption.
